# Content optimization criteria

Load this when deciding what to change in existing content.

## The default is a small edit

Most pages do not need a rewrite. A rewrite discards whatever was working, costs the
most, and is the hardest change to reverse. Prefer, in order: **cut → clarify →
restructure → add → rewrite.** Only propose a rewrite when the page targets the wrong
intent or the substance is absent, and say which.

## Diagnose against the job

The page has a job: a reader arrives with a question or a task. Every edit is
justified by "this helps them do that faster or better". Edits justified by keyword
presence, length or competitor comparison are not justified.

## What actually improves a page

| Change | When it helps | The myth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cutting | Preamble before the answer, repetition, filler that exists to fill | That more words help |
| Front-loading the answer | The question has a direct answer buried below | That the answer must be withheld to keep readers |
| Splitting a wall into passages | Long undifferentiated prose | That formatting alone is optimisation |
| Naming entities explicitly | Pronouns and vague references where a name is clearer | That entities are a density target |
| Fixing heading logic | Headings that style rather than structure | That headings need keywords |
| Adding what is missing | The page does not answer part of what it promises | That adding sections is always progress |
| Concrete specifics | Generic claims anyone could write | That specificity is optional |

## Passage structure and citability

Self-contained passages that answer one question each are easier for readers to scan,
and easier for any system to extract and cite. That means: a question answered where
it is asked, no dependence on distant context, unambiguous references, and claims
stated with their support nearby.

This is good writing, and it is what makes content quotable. It is not a technique for
gaming generative systems, and it should not be framed as one.

## What to remove

Content that repeats what a prior section said, that pads towards a length, that
restates the question without answering, that qualifies to the point of saying
nothing, or that was written for a keyword rather than a reader. Removal is a
legitimate, often high-value recommendation — say so explicitly, since it is
under-proposed.

## What you must not do

Invent facts, statistics, sources, quotes, credentials or first-hand experience.
Where the page needs evidence it does not have, say what is needed and who could
supply it. Never add plausible-sounding specifics to make content feel authoritative.

## Output shape

Locate every edit precisely — the section or passage, what to change, why, and how to
tell it worked. Separate high-confidence corrections from editorial experiments, and
say which are which. An unlocated recommendation is not actionable.
